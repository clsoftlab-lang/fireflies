> [!IMPORTANT]
> **A firefly is not litter.** Leave-no-trace is not an add-on to this project — it is one of its
> founding principles (design spec F4). This guide expands
> [design spec §7](00-design-spec.md#7-leave-no-trace-environmental-design); the spec wins if they
> disagree.

The whole idea is a riverside left exactly as you found it. Every part that flies is chosen to come
down safely and break down in nature, and every show has a recovery plan. There is **no "release
thousands and walk away" mode**, and there never will be.

## 1. The rule: released = recovered

**The number of units you release must equal the number you recover or otherwise account for.**
This one rule drives everything else: it is why we tether by default, why we log every unit, and why
we only free-release biodegradable units in a place where we can collect them.

If you cannot see how you would recover a unit, do not release it.

## 2. Why tethered is best

Tethered (or indoor) operation is the default because it makes leave-no-trace automatic:

- Each unit is on a fine, retrievable line, so **nothing is lost** — recovery is guaranteed, not
  hoped for.
- It also keeps you out of controlled airspace entirely (see
  [03 · Safety & airspace](03-safety-and-airspace.md)).
- It still looks beautiful: the units drift and sway on the wind within the length of their lines,
  and blink into sync exactly the same way.

Free-release is only for open, permitted, low-altitude sites where a full recovery sweep is possible,
using biodegradable units. Treat it as the advanced exception, not the goal.

## 3. Materials

For any free-release, choose parts that come down safely and break down:

| Part | Preferred choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Envelope | **Biodegradable latex** micro-balloon | Breaks down in nature far better than metallised film. Foil holds helium longer but is not biodegradable — use it only tethered-and-recovered. |
| LED carrier | **Cellulose / paper** carrier | Compostable; holds the micro-LED and circuit lightly. |
| Power | **Recoverable or compostable** cell where possible; otherwise smallest cell, fully recovered | Cells are never left in nature — recover and recycle every one. |
| Tendrils | Light, minimal material | Less material to recover; keep strands short and few. |
| Tether (tethered mode) | Fine, strong, retrievable line | The recovery guarantee. |

Metallised film, standard batteries and non-degradable plastics are acceptable **only** in
tethered-and-recovered operation, where every unit comes back.

## 4. Recovery protocol

1. **Before the show:** assign a recovery team, mark the expected drift/recovery area, and prepare
   bags, a log sheet and spare lighting for a dusk sweep.
2. **Tethered mode:** reel each unit back in; confirm each line and unit against the log.
3. **Free-release mode:** track drift, then sweep the downwind recovery area on foot at and after
   landing. Collect every unit, every fragment and every cell.
4. **Cells:** never leave a cell in nature. Bag them separately for battery recycling.
5. **Fragments:** collect balloon fragments and tendrils; latex still must be picked up even though
   it degrades.

## 5. Counting

- **Log every unit** as it is prepared and released (a simple numbered sheet is enough).
- After recovery, **reconcile:** recovered + accounted-for must equal released.
- If a unit is unaccounted for, record it, extend the sweep, and treat it as a lesson for the next
  show (fewer units, more tethering, better tracking). Publish honest counts in your
  [show report](../../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/show-report.yml).

## 6. After the show

- Leave the site cleaner than you found it — pick up unrelated litter too.
- Recycle cells and any recyclable parts; compost the compostable carriers where facilities allow.
- Record what worked and what did not, so the next show recovers even more reliably.
- Share the count and lessons openly. Honest leave-no-trace reporting is part of the art.
